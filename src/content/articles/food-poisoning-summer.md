Food poisoning usually causes nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and stomach cramps, starting anywhere from a few hours to a few days after eating. Most people get better on their own within a few days, and the most important thing is to keep up your fluids with small, frequent sips of water or an oral rehydration solution. See a doctor if you can't keep fluids down for more than a day, your symptoms last more than 3 days, or there's blood or mucus in your vomit or poo.

## Why is food poisoning more common in summer?

The germs that cause food poisoning multiply fastest between 5°C and 60°C, the range food safety authorities call the temperature danger zone. In a Gold Coast summer, food left out at a barbecue, a picnic or a day at the beach warms up quickly, so it reaches that range sooner and stays there longer.

## What are the symptoms of food poisoning?

- nausea and vomiting
- diarrhoea
- stomach pain and cramps
- sometimes a fever, headache or aches

How soon symptoms start depends on the germ. healthdirect gives salmonella as an example: symptoms usually start 6 to 72 hours after eating and last 2 to 5 days. That's why the last meal you ate isn't always the one that made you sick.

## How do I treat food poisoning at home?

1. **Keep up your fluids.** Take small, frequent sips of water. If you're vomiting or have a lot of diarrhoea, an oral rehydration solution from the pharmacy replaces the water and salts you're losing.
2. **Rest,** and eat plain food when you feel ready.
3. **Ask before taking medicines for diarrhoea.** Talk to a pharmacist or doctor first, especially if you have a fever or blood in your poo, and don't give these medicines to children unless a doctor advises it.
4. **Stop it spreading.** Wash your hands well with soap after using the toilet, and don't prepare food for other people while you're unwell. If you work with food, Australian food safety guidance is to stay off food handling until at least 48 hours after your last symptom.

Children dehydrate faster than adults. Our guide to [gastro and dehydration in kids](/gastro-in-kids-hydration/) explains how much fluid to give and the signs to watch for.

## When should I see a doctor?

healthdirect advises contacting a doctor if:

- your symptoms are very severe;
- you can't keep fluids down for more than a day;
- your symptoms last more than 3 days;
- there's blood or mucus in your vomit or poo.

See a doctor sooner if you're pregnant, older, or have a weakened immune system, or if a baby or young child is unwell, because the risk of complications is higher. Watch for signs of dehydration too: feeling very thirsty, a dry mouth, passing only a little dark urine, or feeling dizzy when you stand up.

**Call 000** if someone is very drowsy, confused or collapses.

## How do I keep food safe at a summer barbecue?

### Keep cold food cold

Keep salads, dips, meat and seafood in the fridge until you need them, and carry them to the park or beach in an esky with plenty of ice. Put leftovers back on ice or in the fridge as soon as everyone has eaten.

### Use the 2-hour/4-hour rule

Food Standards Australia New Zealand's 2-hour/4-hour rule was written for food businesses, but it's a useful guide at home too. Add up the total time cooked or ready-to-eat food has spent between 5°C and 60°C:

| Total time out | What to do |
|---|---|
| Under 2 hours | Use it, or put it in the fridge for later |
| 2 to 4 hours | Use it now, and don't put it back in the fridge |
| 4 hours or more | Throw it out |

### Cook meat right through

The Food Safety Information Council says sausages, hamburgers and chicken should reach 75°C in the centre, and that a meat thermometer is the only reliable way to check. Push it into the thickest part. Don't put cooked meat back on the plate that held the raw meat, and keep raw meat away from salads and other food that won't be cooked.

### Wash your hands

Wash your hands with soap before handling food and after touching raw meat. At the beach or park, a bottle of water and some soap, or hand sanitiser, is better than nothing.

## How long do leftovers last?

- **Cool them quickly.** Put leftovers in the fridge within 2 hours of cooking. Dividing them into small, shallow containers helps them cool faster.
- **Eat them soon.** Use most leftovers within 2 to 3 days, and cooked rice and pasta within 2 days.
- **Reheat until steaming hot** all the way through, and only reheat leftovers once.
- **Keep your fridge at 5°C or below.**

## How can our pharmacists help?

Our pharmacists at Pacific Fair can help you choose an oral rehydration solution, check whether your symptoms need a doctor, and work out what to do about your regular medicines. Vomiting and diarrhoea can stop some medicines working properly, including some types of contraception, so ask us if you're not sure. If you have diabetes, follow your sick day plan, and ask us if you don't have one.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

Talk to a pharmacist about rehydration, mild symptoms or your medicines. See a GP if you have any of the warning signs above, or if you're not improving after a couple of days. The GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.
