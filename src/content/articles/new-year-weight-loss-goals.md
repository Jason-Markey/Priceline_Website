A realistic New Year weight loss goal is a modest, steady one. Losing around 5 to 10 per cent of your body weight is widely seen as enough to bring meaningful health benefits, and a systematic review found most studies showed some improvement even with smaller losses. Focus on a few changes you can keep up, and track your progress over months rather than days. Our pharmacists at Pacific Fair offer free, private support and progress checks.

## What's a realistic weight loss goal?

Five to 10 per cent of your body weight is a common target. For someone who weighs 90 kg, that's around 4.5 to 9 kg. It may sound small, but the systematic review notes this range is often considered clinically meaningful for health, and that guidelines in the UK and the United States recommend modest weight loss in this range.

Your weight goes up and down from day to day with fluids, food and exercise, so weigh yourself at the same time of day, once a week at most, and look at the trend over a month.

## Small changes that add up

### Eating

The Australian Dietary Guidelines recommend a wide variety of foods from the five food groups every day: vegetables and legumes, fruit, mostly wholegrain cereals, lean protein, and milk, yoghurt and cheese, plus plenty of water. Practical swaps that make a difference:

- Fill half your plate with vegetables.
- Swap sugary drinks for water.
- Plan meals and snacks for the week, so you're not relying on takeaway.
- Watch portion sizes, and eat slowly.
- Limit alcohol, which adds kilojoules quickly.

### Activity

Australia's physical activity guidelines recommend adults do 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity, such as brisk walking, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity each week, plus muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days. Start where you are and build up. On the Gold Coast, an early-morning walk along the beach or a swim between the flags beats the midday heat.

### Sleep and stress

Regular, good-quality sleep and ways to manage stress can make the other changes easier to stick with.

## Track your progress for free

Our in-store [Health Station](/health-station/) can record your weight, BMI and blood pressure for free and email you the results, so you can see your progress over time. Checking in every few weeks keeps you motivated and shows changes the bathroom scales miss, such as your blood pressure.

## Can my medicines affect my weight?

Some can. Some medicines can increase your appetite or cause weight gain, and others need extra care if you change how much you eat. Our pharmacists can check your current medicines and talk to your doctor if something might need to change. Don't stop a prescribed medicine without talking to your doctor first. If you take several medicines, a [medication review](/medication-reviews/) can check they're working well together.

## What about meal replacement shakes?

Meal replacement shakes don't suit everyone. Ask a pharmacist before you start one, especially if you have a health condition, take medicines for diabetes, or are pregnant or breastfeeding.

## When to see a GP

See your GP if you have unexplained weight loss or gain, a health condition such as diabetes or heart disease, or if you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy. Your GP can also talk to you about other options and ongoing support. The GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.
