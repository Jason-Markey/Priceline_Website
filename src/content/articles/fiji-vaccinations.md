You don't need any vaccines to enter Fiji from Australia, but a few are well worth having. Make sure you're up to date with measles (MMR), tetanus and flu, and talk to a pharmacist about hepatitis A and typhoid, which spread through food and water. Smartraveller also advises a meningococcal vaccine. Fiji has no malaria and is recognised as rabies-free, but dengue is a risk, so pack insect repellent. Try to start 6 to 8 weeks before you fly.

## Which vaccines do I need for Fiji?

It depends on your health, how long you're staying and what you're planning, so treat this as a starting point for a chat with a pharmacist or doctor.

| Vaccine | Who should consider it for Fiji | Can we give it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) | Anyone born in or after 1966 without 2 documented doses | Yes, and it's free in Queensland if you were born in or after 1966 and haven't had 2 doses |
| Tetanus, diphtheria and whooping cough | Anyone whose last tetanus booster was more than 10 years ago | Yes |
| Flu | Worth considering for all travellers, and especially relevant on a cruise | Yes |
| COVID-19 | Check whether you're due, especially before a cruise. It's free for people aged 65 and over and some other groups | Yes |
| Hepatitis A | Recommended for travellers to places where hepatitis A is found, which includes Fiji | Yes |
| Typhoid | Some travellers, such as people visiting friends and relatives, or staying where food and water may not be safe | Yes |
| Hepatitis B | Long or frequent trips, or if you might need medical or dental treatment | Yes |
| Meningococcal | Smartraveller advises getting vaccinated before you travel | Yes |
| Rabies | Not usually needed, as Fiji is recognised as rabies-free | No. We'll point you to a GP or travel clinic |
| Dengue | A vaccine is available in Australia, but it isn't suitable for everyone | No. We'll point you to a GP or travel clinic |
| Yellow fever | Not needed when you travel from Australia | No |

Most travel vaccines aren't free, so a private fee usually applies. You'll see current prices when you book online. If a vaccine is free for you under the National Immunisation Program or a Queensland Government program, there's no fee.

## Measles: check you've had 2 doses

Smartraveller notes that Fiji has had a measles outbreak in the past. Two doses of MMR vaccine give lifelong protection against measles to 99 in 100 people. If you were born in or after 1966 and don't have records of 2 doses, you can have it free in Queensland. NSW Health advises having the final dose at least 2 weeks before you leave.

After you get home, watch for measles symptoms for 3 weeks: fever, cough, runny nose, sore red eyes and a spotty rash that starts on the head and neck. If they appear, call your GP or the emergency department before you go in, and mention your trip.

## Hepatitis A and typhoid

Both spread through contaminated food and water, and Smartraveller says foodborne and waterborne illnesses are common in Fiji.

- **Hepatitis A.** The Government of Canada says there's a risk of hepatitis A in Fiji, and recommends the vaccine for all travellers to places where it's found. One dose gives protection, and a second dose 6 to 12 months later makes it last longer.
- **Typhoid.** Smartraveller says typhoid cases have been reported in Fiji, though Canada rates the risk as low for most travellers. The Australian Immunisation Handbook recommends the vaccine for travel to places where typhoid is found if food hygiene or drinking water may not be up to standard, or to visit friends and relatives. It's a single injection, or capsules taken over several days (from age 6). The injection is estimated to be about 65% effective over 3 years, so careful eating and drinking still matter.

## Hepatitis B and meningococcal disease

- **Hepatitis B.** The Handbook lists Pacific island countries among the places where hepatitis B is common, and recommends the vaccine for long or frequent trips. Travellers can be exposed through blood, including during emergency medical or dental treatment, or through sex. Smartraveller also notes the Fiji Government has declared an HIV outbreak, so take precautions with anything that could expose you to infection.
- **Meningococcal disease** can often be fatal, and Smartraveller advises getting vaccinated before you go. Ask us which vaccine suits you. If you notice a high fever, sensitivity to light or nausea while you're away, get medical help straight away.

## Dengue and mosquito bites

Smartraveller says Fiji is prone to outbreaks of mosquito-borne illnesses, and the Government of Canada lists dengue, Zika and chikungunya as risks there. In September 2026, the World Health Organization reported an increase in dengue-like illness in Fiji. The mosquitoes that spread dengue bite during the day, especially around dawn and dusk.

A dengue vaccine is registered in Australia for people aged 4 and over, given as 2 doses 3 months apart. It's mainly recommended for people who've had dengue before, or who'll spend long or repeated periods where dengue is common. It isn't suitable for everyone, including people with a weakened immune system, and it isn't recommended during pregnancy. We don't give it, so ask a GP or travel doctor whether it suits you.

Vaccinated or not, avoiding bites matters:

- Use an insect repellent containing DEET, picaridin or oil of lemon eucalyptus, and reapply it as directed.
- Wear loose, light-coloured clothing that covers your arms and legs.
- Choose rooms with screens or air-conditioning.

Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

If you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy, see your doctor before you go, as Zika can cause serious birth defects. While you're away, get medical help if you have a fever, muscle pain, a rash or a severe headache. Before you leave, check Smartraveller and Fiji's Ministry of Health and Medical Services for current outbreaks.

## Leptospirosis: take care after floods

Smartraveller says leptospirosis cases have been reported in Fiji. It spreads through water or soil contaminated with animal urine, getting in through cuts and broken skin or when you swallow it. The Government of Canada says the risk often rises after a cyclone or flood, and Fiji's cyclone season runs from November to April, which takes in the Christmas holidays. There's no vaccine for people, so avoid flooded rivers and creeks, don't swim or wade in water that could be contaminated, cover cuts with a waterproof dressing, and wear footwear outdoors.

Symptoms are usually flu-like, such as fever, a severe headache and sore muscles. If you have them, or think you've been exposed, tell your doctor.

## Is there malaria or rabies in Fiji?

- **Malaria: no.** The World Health Organization lists Fiji among the countries where malaria never existed or disappeared without specific measures, so you don't need malaria tablets for Fiji.
- **Rabies:** the Biosecurity Authority of Fiji says Fiji is recognised as a rabies-free country, so most travellers won't need the vaccine. The Government of Canada notes rabies may be present in some wildlife, including bats. If a bat or other wild animal bites or scratches you, wash the wound with soap and water for about 15 minutes and get medical care as soon as possible.

## Food, water and drinks

The Government of Canada says travellers' diarrhoea is the most common illness affecting travellers. To lower your risk:

- If you're not sure the water is safe, drink sealed bottled water and use it to brush your teeth.
- Skip ice unless you know it's made from safe water.
- Eat food that's freshly cooked and served hot, and peel fruit yourself.
- Wash your hands with soap and water before you eat and after the toilet.

If you get diarrhoea, drink plenty of fluids and use oral rehydration solution. Smartraveller advises getting medical advice if you have a fever or diarrhoea.

Smartraveller also warns that kava (yaqona) can be dangerous if it reacts with alcohol or prescription medicines, including antidepressants, so check with your pharmacist first if you take regular medicines. Natural toxins in seafood can also make you ill. Methanol in drinks is another risk: "As little as one shot can be fatal." Only drink branded, sealed drinks, and get urgent medical help if you think you've been poisoned.

Pack a small travel health kit with oral rehydration solution, pain relief, antiseptic, insect repellent and sunscreen. Our [travel health checklist](/travel-health-checklist/) has a full countdown to your trip.

## Sun safety

Cancer Council NSW explains that closer to the equator the sun sits higher in the sky, so UV passes through less of the atmosphere that absorbs it, and that water and sand reflect UV. Cancer Council Australia recommends SPF50 or SPF50+ broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen, applied 20 minutes before you go out and reapplied every 2 hours and after swimming, sweating or towel-drying, along with a broad-brimmed hat, sun-protective clothing, sunglasses and shade. See our [Gold Coast sun safety guide](/sun-safety-gold-coast/) for more.

## Going on a South Pacific cruise?

If your cruise from Brisbane stops in Fiji, everything above applies when you go ashore. Life on board adds a few things.

- **Flu and COVID-19.** Smartraveller says infectious diseases like COVID-19, flu and gastro are common on cruise ships, and the Australian Immunisation Handbook says the flu vaccine is particularly relevant for cruises. Check whether you're due for both before you sail.
- **Gastro.** Wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds before eating and after the toilet. Use hand sanitiser when soap and water aren't available.
- **Medical care.** Smartraveller warns that standards on board may not be as good as in Australia, and you may need to pay medical bills straight away. Choose travel insurance that covers cruising, shore excursions and medical evacuation at sea.
- **Every port is different.** Read the travel advice for each stop. For example, the Government of Canada lists malaria as a risk in Vanuatu. Bring your itinerary in and we'll go through each port with you, or see a GP or travel doctor. If you need malaria tablets, which need a prescription, [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can help.
- **Your medicines.** Pack enough to cover any delays, and if you have a health condition, talk to your doctor about your cruise plans before you commit.

## Medical care and travel insurance

Smartraveller says medical care in Fiji is limited, and if you become seriously ill or have an accident you may need to be evacuated to Australia, which can be very expensive. Get comprehensive travel insurance that covers medical evacuation, and diving if you plan to dive.

## How far ahead should I start?

Ideally 6 to 8 weeks before you fly, as some vaccines need more than one dose or take a couple of weeks to work. Flying over the Christmas holidays? Count back from your departure date and book early. Leaving sooner? Come in anyway. A single dose of hepatitis A vaccine still gives protection, and some courses can be sped up.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

Talk to your pharmacist about which vaccines suit your trip, what to pack, and how to take your regular medicines with you.

See a GP or travel doctor before you go if you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy, have a weakened immune system or a long-term health condition, are travelling with children under 5, or need dengue vaccine or malaria tablets.

After you get home, see a doctor if you have a fever, a rash or diarrhoea that doesn't settle, and tell them you've been to Fiji. In an emergency, call 000.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need vaccinations to travel to Fiji?

None are required to enter Fiji from Australia. But being up to date with measles, tetanus and flu, and talking to a pharmacist about hepatitis A and typhoid, is recommended for most travellers.

### How long before Fiji should I get vaccinated?

Ideally 6 to 8 weeks before you fly. If you're leaving sooner, come in anyway. Some protection is better than none.

### Do I need the rabies vaccine or malaria tablets for Fiji?

Usually not. Fiji is recognised as rabies-free and has no malaria. If your trip includes other countries, check those too.

### What vaccines do I need for a South Pacific cruise?

Check your routine vaccines, and whether you're due for flu and COVID-19, as both are common on cruise ships. Then check each port, as places like Vanuatu carry different risks from Fiji.
