## Australian vitamins and supplements

Popular choices include multivitamins, fish oil, probiotics, magnesium, vitamin D, calcium, and vitamins for pregnancy and for children. Brands we stock include Blackmores, Swisse, BioCeuticals, Nature’s Way, Nature’s Own, Bioglan, Herbs of Gold, Ethical Nutrients, Cenovis and Ostelin. Ranges vary, so ask us if you’re after something specific, or see our [vitamins and supplements](/vitamins-supplements/) page.

Before you buy:

- **Look for the AUST L or AUST R number on the label.** It shows the product is entered on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods and can be legally sold in Australia. Most vitamins are AUST L, the low-risk “listed” category.
- **Check the expiry date,** especially if you’re buying gifts or stocking up for months ahead. We’re happy to check for you.
- **Ask a pharmacist first** if the vitamins are for someone who is pregnant or breastfeeding, a child, an older person, or anyone who takes prescription medicines, because some supplements interact with medicines. Advice is free and you don’t need to book. Our guide to [supplements that interact with medicines](/supplements-that-interact-with-medicines/) explains more.
- **Buying more doesn’t mean taking more.** Take them as the label says, and don’t go over the recommended dose.

Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## Lucas’ Papaw Ointment

Lucas’ Papaw Ointment, which many people search for as “Lucas paw paw”, is a Queensland classic. It was developed more than a century ago by Brisbane doctor Thomas Pennington Lucas, and his family’s company still makes it in Brisbane today. Its key ingredient is fermented papaw (papaya).

The label lists uses including minor burns, cuts, insect bites, nappy rash and chafing. It comes in sizes from a small lip applicator tube to a large jar, so it’s easy to buy as small gifts.

- For a minor burn, cool it under cool running water for 20 minutes first. Our guide to [treating a cut or scrape](/how-to-treat-a-cut/) covers first aid for small wounds.
- See a doctor for a serious burn or wound, or if a sore looks infected.

Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## Lanolin cream

Lanolin is a natural wax from sheep’s wool, and Australian lanolin creams are a popular moisturiser and gift. They’re rich and thick, so a little goes a long way, especially on dry hands, elbows and feet.

Lanolin allergy is uncommon, but it’s more likely if you have eczema or broken skin. Try a small amount on a patch of skin first, and stop using it if your skin becomes red or itchy.

## Claiming the GST back: the Tourist Refund Scheme

If you’re leaving Australia within 60 days, you can usually claim back the GST (goods and services tax) on what you buy through the Tourist Refund Scheme (TRS):

- Spend AU$300 or more (GST included) with us, in one purchase or several.
- Ask for a tax invoice when you pay. For purchases of $1,000 or more, the invoice needs your full name as it appears on your passport.
- Keep the goods unopened and carry them in your hand luggage. Larger liquids, aerosols and gels can go in checked luggage, but show them to the Australian Border Force before you check in.
- Make your claim at the TRS counter in the international departure area of the airport, such as Gold Coast or Brisbane Airport. Claims close 30 minutes before departure, so leave plenty of time. You can enter your details in the TRS app beforehand to save time.
- You can only claim back GST you paid. Most vitamins, skincare and fragrance include GST, while prescription medicines don’t.

The full rules are on the [Australian Border Force website](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/tourist-refund-scheme).

## Packing for your flight

- Creams and ointments count as liquids at airport security. On international flights, containers in your hand luggage are usually limited to 100 mL each, so pack larger jars in your checked luggage. Check your airline’s rules before you fly.
- Keep vitamins in their original packaging with the label on, which helps if a customs officer asks about them.
