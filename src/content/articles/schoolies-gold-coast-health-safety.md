Gold Coast Schoolies Week runs from 21 to 27 November 2026, centred on Surfers Paradise. Most of what keeps school leavers safe is simple: pack sun protection and water, stick with your mates, keep an eye on your drink, and know where to get help if something goes wrong. Here's a pharmacist's checklist for the week, whether you're going yourself or packing a bag for someone who is.

## When is Schoolies on the Gold Coast in 2026?

The Queensland Government's Safer Schoolies website lists Gold Coast Schoolies Week as 21 to 27 November 2026. Year 12 school leavers can register online for a free official wristband, which you need to get into the Schoolies Hub on Surfers Paradise beach. In past years the Hub has been a fenced, alcohol- and drug-free area. Check [saferschoolies.qld.gov.au](https://www.saferschoolies.qld.gov.au/) for this year's details before you go.

## What should I pack for Schoolies?

- **Sun protection:** an SPF50 or SPF50+ broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses. Our [sunscreen checklist](/best-sunscreen-gold-coast/) explains what to look for.
- **Water:** a refillable bottle, plus a few oral rehydration sachets for hot days.
- **Your regular medicines,** in their original packaging, and anything you carry for emergencies, such as an asthma puffer. If you have [eScripts](/how-to-use-escripts/), keep the tokens handy so any pharmacy can fill them.
- **Condoms.**
- **A small first aid kit:** plasters, blister plasters for new shoes, antiseptic, and pain relief you know suits you. Our [first aid kit checklist](/first-aid-kit-checklist/) has the full list.
- **Insect repellent** for evenings outside.
- **A charged phone and a power bank,** with your emergency contacts saved and your accommodation address written down.
- **Your official wristband** and photo ID.

Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## How do I stay safe in the sun and heat?

UV on the Gold Coast is already very high to extreme in November, and a bad sunburn on the first day can spoil the rest of the week. Put sunscreen on before you head out, and reapply it every two hours and after swimming, sweating or towelling off. Take breaks in the shade in the middle of the day, and drink water regularly, even if you're not thirsty.

Know the signs of heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, dizziness, a headache, nausea and feeling very weak. Get into the shade or air-con, cool down and sip water. Heatstroke is an emergency: if a friend becomes confused, collapses or has a seizure, call 000. Our guide to [heat exhaustion and heatstroke](/heat-exhaustion-symptoms/) explains first aid step by step.

Swim between the red and yellow flags, never swim after drinking, and watch out for bluebottles. Our [bluebottle sting guide](/bluebottle-sting-treatment/) explains what to do if you're stung.

## How can I look after my mates?

- **Make a plan together** before you head out: where you're going, how you're getting home and where to meet if you get separated.
- **Stick together,** and don't leave anyone behind, especially someone who's very drunk or unwell.
- **Keep your phones charged** and share your locations with each other.
- **If someone can't be woken, is breathing strangely or is vomiting while drowsy,** put them on their side and call 000 straight away. Stay with them until help arrives.

## What are the signs of drink spiking?

Drink spiking, when someone adds alcohol or drugs to another person's drink without their consent, is a serious criminal offence in Queensland. Queensland Police list warning signs such as feeling dizzy, sick or much more affected by alcohol than you'd expect, sudden drowsiness or confusion, and a drink that tastes, looks or smells different.

Keep your drink with you, watch it being poured and don't accept an open drink from anyone. If you or a friend suddenly feel very unwell, tell someone you trust straight away, don't leave the person alone, and call 000 in an emergency.

## What if I'm drinking?

It's against the law to buy alcohol if you're under 18. If you're 18 and choose to drink, eat first, alternate alcoholic drinks with water, and keep count, because it's easy to lose track on a big night. Never drive or swim after drinking. If you're feeling rough the next morning, our guide to [hangover remedies](/hangover-remedies/) covers what actually helps.

## Sexual health: condoms and emergency contraception

Condoms are the only contraception that also protects against sexually transmitted infections (STIs), so pack them even if you don't plan to need them. Sex should always be with consent, and someone who is very drunk can't give it.

If a condom breaks or you have sex without contraception, the emergency contraceptive pill (the morning-after pill) is available from a pharmacist without a prescription. It works best the sooner you take it, ideally within 72 hours, and depending on the type it can be taken up to 5 days afterwards. **Ask your pharmacist about this product.** Our [emergency contraception](/emergency-contraception/) page explains how it works at Pacific Fair, with no appointment or consultation fee.

If you notice unusual discharge, pain when you wee, sores or a rash, or you're worried about an STI, see a GP or a sexual health clinic for a check.

## Where can I get help during Schoolies?

- **Emergencies:** call 000 for an ambulance, the police or the fire service.
- **The Safer Schoolies response:** in past years, the official response in Surfers Paradise has included first aid and welfare services, and volunteers such as Red Frogs have supported school leavers. Check [saferschoolies.qld.gov.au](https://www.saferschoolies.qld.gov.au/) for this year's details.
- **Health advice at any time:** call 13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84) to talk to a Queensland Health nurse.
- **Someone to talk to:** Lifeline on 13 11 14, or Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800 if you're 25 or under.
- **A pharmacy:** we're at Pacific Fair in Broadbeach, open 7 days and a short trip from Surfers Paradise on the light rail (get off at Broadbeach South). Our pharmacists can help with sunburn, blisters, cuts, an upset stomach and emergency contraception.
- **A doctor:** the GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.

## For parents: helping them get ready

- Pack the basics above together, and talk through the plan for the week, including how they'll get home each night.
- Make sure they have their regular medicines and know how to use anything they carry for emergencies.
- Agree on a simple way to check in, such as a daily message.
- Remind them they can always call you, or 000, if something goes wrong.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

Talk to a pharmacist about mild sunburn, blisters, minor cuts and grazes, an upset stomach or emergency contraception. See a GP if:

- sunburn blisters over a large area, or comes with a fever, chills or vomiting
- a cut or blister looks infected, with increasing redness, swelling, warmth or pus
- you have symptoms of an STI, or you're worried after unprotected sex
- you're still feeling unwell after a day or two

**Call 000 in an emergency.** That includes someone who can't be woken, is having trouble breathing, has a seizure or shows signs of heatstroke.
