If you're unwell after hours near Broadbeach, call triple zero (000) in an emergency. If it isn't an emergency but you're worried, call 13 HEALTH on 13 43 25 84. A registered nurse answers any time, day or night, and can tell you how urgent it is and where to get care.

Knowing who to call first can save a wasted drive at night. Here's where to go, who to call, and what to check before you leave home.

## When should I call 000?

Call 000 for an ambulance when someone's life may be in danger. healthdirect lists these as emergencies that need 000 or a hospital emergency department, not an urgent care clinic:

- chest pain or tightness
- breathing difficulties
- uncontrollable bleeding
- severe burns
- poisoning
- seizures
- ongoing fever in infants

Swelling of the tongue or throat, noisy breathing or collapse can be signs of anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction. The Queensland Government says anaphylaxis is a medical emergency, so call 000 immediately.

## Where is the nearest emergency department?

Gold Coast Health runs two public emergency departments, and says they operate 24 hours a day:

- **Gold Coast University Hospital**, Southport
- **Robina Hospital**, Robina

When you arrive, a nurse assesses how urgent your condition is. This is called triage. Gold Coast Health explains that the sickest patients receive care first, regardless of when they arrived.

Tell the triage nurse about any medicines you take and any health conditions you have. A written list of your medicines makes this easier. In an emergency, Gold Coast Health's advice is to call 000 and ask for an ambulance.

## Not an emergency? Call 13 HEALTH

13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84) is the Queensland Government's confidential phone health advice line. Registered nurses answer 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

The nurse will:

- assess your symptoms over the phone
- estimate how urgent they are, rather than diagnose the cause
- recommend when and where to get care
- refer you to other health care providers if needed

That makes 13 HEALTH a good first call when you're not sure whether something can wait until morning.

13 HEALTH is only available in Queensland. Outside Queensland, call healthdirect on 1800 022 222. healthdirect says its registered nurses are also available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

## What is a Medicare Urgent Care Clinic?

Medicare Urgent Care Clinics are for illnesses and injuries that are urgent but not life-threatening. healthdirect says they're usually run by GPs, and you don't need an appointment or referral.

According to healthdirect, they can treat problems such as:

- minor infections, including urinary tract infections
- mild burns
- small cuts needing stitches or glue
- insect bites, stings and rashes
- minor eye and ear problems
- minor fractures, sprains and back pain

The Australian Government says Medicare Urgent Care Clinics are bulk billed. healthdirect advises bringing your Medicare card if you have one.

On the Gold Coast, there are Medicare Urgent Care Clinics in Southport and Oxenford. Use the [Australian Government's Medicare Urgent Care Clinic finder](https://www.health.gov.au/find-a-medicare-ucc) to check the nearest clinic and its hours before you go.

The Queensland Government's Tugun Satellite Hospital also has a walk-in Minor Injury and Illness Clinic for urgent illnesses and injuries that aren't life-threatening. Check its hours before you go.

## Who else can I call?

### Poisons Information Centre: 13 11 26

Call 13 11 26 for advice about poisoning, including medicine overdoses, household chemicals, bites and stings, and poisonous plants or mushrooms. The Queensland Poisons Information Centre gives advice 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, from anywhere in Australia.

If the person is having a seizure or trouble breathing, call 000.

### Lifeline: 13 11 14

If you're in emotional distress or thinking about suicide, call Lifeline on 13 11 14. Lifeline offers confidential crisis support 24/7, and you can also text or chat online. If life is in danger, call 000.

## How can you be ready before you need help?

- **Save the numbers.** Put 13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84) and the Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26) in your phone.
- **Carry a medicines list.** The Better Health Channel suggests carrying a list of your medicines in case of an emergency.
- **Bring your Medicare card** if you have one, even on holiday.
- **Pack a first aid kit.** Our [first aid kit checklist](/first-aid-kit-checklist/) covers the basics.
- **Check our hours before you travel**, especially around public holidays.

## How our pharmacists can help

During opening hours, our pharmacists can help with many minor illnesses and injuries, and tell you when you need a doctor instead. You don't need an appointment, and anyone can ask to talk to a pharmacist privately. See [what a pharmacist can treat in Queensland](/what-can-a-pharmacist-treat-queensland/).

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

See a pharmacist or GP during opening hours if:

- a minor illness or injury isn't getting better
- you've run out of a regular medicine, or are about to
- you need a new script
- you're not sure whether something needs a doctor

Call 13 HEALTH at any hour if you're unsure how urgent it is. If someone has taken too much of a medicine, or the wrong one, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.

**Call 000 in an emergency.** If someone has chest pain, trouble breathing, uncontrollable bleeding, a seizure or signs of a severe allergic reaction, don't wait. Call triple zero (000).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a Medicare card for an urgent care clinic?

healthdirect says to bring your Medicare card if you have one. If you don't, it suggests checking whether you're eligible, and how to apply, on the Services Australia website.

### Can I go to an emergency department for something minor?

Emergency departments are for serious illness and injury, and the sickest patients are seen first. Gold Coast Health says other options are available for non-urgent conditions. Call 13 HEALTH if you're not sure where to go.
