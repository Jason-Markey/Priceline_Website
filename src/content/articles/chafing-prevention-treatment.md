Chafing is red, sore, stinging skin caused by rubbing, and sweat and humidity make it worse, which is why it's so common in a Gold Coast summer. Most chafing settles within a few days once you stop the rubbing and keep the skin clean, dry and protected. To prevent it, wear breathable or moisture-wicking clothes that fit well, put a barrier balm or petroleum jelly on spots that rub, and change out of wet or sweaty clothes as soon as you can.

## What causes chafing?

Chafing happens when skin rubs against skin or clothing, especially when it's damp. Healthline describes it as a combination of friction, moisture and irritating fabric. Common spots are:

- the inner thighs
- the underarms
- the groin and bottom
- under the breasts
- the nipples, especially for runners

A Gold Coast summer stacks the odds: hours of sweat in humid air, wet swimwear, sand, long days walking around the theme parks, and tight or rough fabrics. Shaving stubble can add to the rubbing too.

## How can I prevent chafing?

- **Choose breathable or moisture-wicking fabrics** that draw sweat away from your skin, and clothes that fit well without rubbing.
- **Wear bike shorts or anti-chafe shorts** under dresses and skirts if your thighs rub together.
- **Use a barrier on hot spots.** An anti-chafe balm or stick, or a thin layer of petroleum jelly, cuts down friction before exercise or a long walk.
- **Keep skin folds dry.** A light dusting of powder can help in areas that get sweaty.
- **Change out of wet swimwear and sweaty gym gear** as soon as you can, and rinse off salt and sand.
- **Runners:** protect your nipples with a dab of petroleum jelly or a plaster before a long run.

## How do I treat chafed skin?

1. **Stop the rubbing.** Change into loose, soft clothing, and give the area a break from whatever caused it.
2. **Clean it gently.** Rinse with lukewarm water and a soap-free wash, then pat dry. Don't scrub.
3. **Protect it.** Apply a barrier cream, such as one containing zinc oxide, or petroleum jelly, or a soothing fragrance-free balm.
4. **Let it breathe.** Air the skin when you can, and skip perfumed products on the sore area until it heals.

Mild chafing usually heals within a couple of days once the cause is removed. Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## Is it chafing, heat rash or something else?

- **Chafing** is red, raw or stinging skin in places that rub.
- **Heat rash** (prickly heat) is a rash of tiny, itchy bumps caused by blocked sweat ducts. It usually settles if you keep cool and dry. Our guide to [skincare for humid weather](/skincare-humid-weather/) has more.
- **Intertrigo** is a red, often moist rash in skin folds, such as under the breasts, in the groin or between the thighs. DermNet lists friction, sweat and moisture among its causes. It can become infected with yeast or bacteria, which needs different treatment.

## Chafing in babies and young children

Babies and toddlers can get chafing in skin folds and around the nappy area, especially in hot weather. Keep the area clean and dry, change nappies often, and use a barrier cream: DermNet recommends a protective barrier cream, such as zinc oxide, for nappy rash. See a GP if the rash spreads, blisters, looks infected or isn't improving.

## How can our pharmacists help?

Our pharmacists can help you choose an anti-chafe balm, barrier cream, soap-free wash or powder, and take a look at a rash that isn't settling to check whether it might be infected. If it looks like a yeast infection in a skin fold, they can suggest an antifungal cream if it's suitable, or let you know when it's time to see a GP.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

See a GP ([Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, is one option) if:

- the skin is broken, bleeding, crusted or oozing
- there's increasing redness, swelling, warmth or pus, or you have a fever
- a rash in a skin fold doesn't improve after about a week of care
- you have diabetes or a weakened immune system and the skin is broken
