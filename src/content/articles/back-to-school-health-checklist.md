Queensland state schools go back on Wednesday 27 January 2027. A few health jobs in the fortnight before make the first weeks of term much easier: get any medicines and action plans the school needs up to date, check your child's vaccinations, pack sun protection and a lunchbox that stays cool, and ease bedtimes back to school nights. Here's a pharmacist's checklist from our team at Pacific Fair.

## When does school go back in Queensland in 2027?

The Queensland Department of Education lists Wednesday 27 January 2027 as the first day of Term 1 at state schools. Term 1 runs until Thursday 25 March. Independent and Catholic schools set their own dates, so check with your school.

## The checklist at a glance

- Medicines for school are in their original pharmacy-labelled packaging and in date, and you've filled in the school's consent form.
- The school has your child's current asthma or anaphylaxis action plan, if they have one.
- Vaccinations are up to date, and you've returned any Year 7 or Year 10 consent forms.
- A broad-brimmed, bucket or legionnaire hat, and sunscreen in the school bag.
- An insulated lunchbox with an ice brick or frozen water bottle.
- Bedtimes moved earlier in the week before school.
- Hair checked for head lice.
- New school shoes worn in, and a school bag that fits.

## What does the school need for my child's medicines?

Queensland state schools need authorisation from a doctor, dentist or other prescriber before they can give most medicines, apart from emergency first aid such as an asthma reliever. The medicine needs to be in its original container with the pharmacy label showing your child's name, the dose and when to take it, and it needs to be in date. The school will also ask you to fill in a consent form.

If the dose has changed since the label was printed, the school will need a letter from the doctor explaining the change. Ask us to update the pharmacy label too; we may need to check the new dose with the doctor first.

**Asthma.** Give the school an up-to-date asthma action plan from your GP, and check that your child's reliever puffer and spacer are in date and labelled with their name.

**Allergies and anaphylaxis.** Give the school your child's current ASCIA action plan and their adrenaline injector, and check its expiry date. If your child has a new allergy, see your GP before term starts so the school has a plan from day one.

**Regular medicines at home.** Check how many repeats are left on your child's scripts, and refill them before term gets busy. If you have [eScripts](/how-to-use-escripts/), send them to us ahead and they'll be ready when you arrive.

## Are your child's vaccinations up to date?

The Queensland School Immunisation Program offers free vaccines at school:

| School year | Vaccine | Doses |
|---|---|---|
| Year 7 | HPV | 1 |
| Year 7 | Diphtheria, tetanus and whooping cough | 1 |
| Year 10 | Meningococcal ACWY | 1 |
| Year 10 | Meningococcal B | 2 |

Consent information comes through the school early in the year, so look out for it and send it back. If your teenager missed a school vaccine, free catch-ups are available for teenagers and young adults: see [free vaccines at a Queensland pharmacy](/free-vaccines-pharmacy-queensland/). Our pharmacist immunisers vaccinate people aged 5 and over, or your GP can help.

You can download your child's immunisation history statement from your Medicare online account or the Express Plus Medicare app.

## Sun protection at school

In Queensland the UV Index reaches 3 or above all year round, so children need sun protection at school every day, not just in summer. Cancer Council Queensland recommends:

- a hat that shades the face, neck and ears, such as a broad-brimmed, bucket or legionnaire hat (caps and visors don't protect enough);
- SPF50 or SPF50+ broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen;
- sun-protective clothing, shade and sunglasses.

Put sunscreen on before school, and pack a small tube in the school bag so your child can top up. Our [sunscreen checklist](/best-sunscreen-gold-coast/) explains what to look for. Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## How do I keep a lunchbox cool?

The Food Safety Information Council recommends an insulated lunchbox with an ice brick or frozen water bottle inside, kept in the school bag. Pack lunches the night before and keep them in the fridge until you leave home. On hot days, foods such as hard cheese, canned tuna and sandwich spreads are safer choices, and any meat, egg or sushi that comes home uneaten should go in the bin.

## How much sleep do school-age children need?

Australia's 24-hour movement guidelines recommend 9 to 11 hours of uninterrupted sleep a night for children aged 5 to 13, and 8 to 10 hours for teenagers aged 14 to 17, with consistent bed and wake-up times.

After weeks of late summer nights, try moving bedtime and wake-up time earlier by 15 minutes every night or two in the week before school. Switching screens off well before bed can help too.

## Check for head lice before day one

Head lice spread easily once children are back together in class. Check your child's hair before school starts with conditioner and a fine-tooth comb, then keep checking every week. Our guide to [head lice treatment](/head-lice-treatment/) explains how.

## School bags and shoes

- **School bags.** Queensland Department of Education advice is to keep a backpack under 10% of your child's body weight where possible. Have your child wear it on both shoulders, with the heaviest things packed closest to their back.
- **New school shoes.** Have your child wear new shoes around the house for a few days before school, and pack a couple of blister plasters for the first week.

## Should my child have an eye test?

It's recommended that all children have their eyes checked by an optometrist before they start school, then every 2 to 3 years through school, or sooner if you notice a problem. You don't need a referral. Squinting, holding books very close, headaches or trouble seeing the board are all worth getting checked.

## When to see a GP

See your GP before term starts if your child needs a new or updated asthma or anaphylaxis action plan, has a new allergy, or needs a letter for the school about a change of dose. It's also worth a visit if you're worried about their sleep, eyesight or general health.
