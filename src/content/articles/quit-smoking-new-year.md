The simplest way to quit smoking in the New Year is to make 1 January, or a day soon after, your quit day, prepare in the week before, and line up support: nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), a plan for cravings, and Quitline on 13 7848. Combining support with NRT gives you a better chance of staying smoke-free than going it alone, and our pharmacists at Pacific Fair can help you get started for free.

## Step 1: Choose your quit day

The New Year is a natural fresh start, but the holidays can also be full of triggers: parties, drinks and time away from your usual routine. If 1 January feels too soon, pick a day in the first week or two of January when you'll be less stressed, and write it down. Quitline counsellors can help you choose a date and build a quit plan around you.

Tell your friends and family the date, and ask them not to smoke around you.

## Step 2: Get ready in the week before

- **Clear out cigarettes, lighters and ashtrays** from your home, car and workplace the day before.
- **List your triggers,** such as your morning coffee, a drink after work or a stressful phone call, and plan what you'll do instead.
- **Talk to a pharmacist about NRT,** so you have it on hand from day one.
- **Download the free My QuitBuddy app,** and request a Quitline callback at [quit.org.au](https://www.quit.org.au/).

## Step 3: Choose your nicotine replacement

NRT gives you a lower, steady dose of nicotine without the smoke, so you can deal with withdrawal while you break the habit. It comes as patches, gum, lozenges, mouth sprays and inhalators. Many people use a patch for steady background nicotine plus a faster-acting form for sudden cravings. Most people use NRT for 8 to 12 weeks, then step down gradually.

Your pharmacist can help you choose the type and strength. Some NRT products are subsidised on the PBS when prescribed by a doctor, so ask about the cheapest way to get them. Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## Step 4: Handle cravings with the 4Ds

Cravings are strongest in the first few weeks, but each one only lasts a few minutes. The Better Health Channel suggests the 4Ds:

- **Delay** acting on the urge for a few minutes, and it will pass.
- **Deep breaths:** breathe slowly in and out.
- **Drink water,** sipping it slowly.
- **Do something else** to take your mind off it, such as going for a walk.

## What to expect in the first few weeks

Cravings, irritability, restlessness, trouble concentrating and poor sleep are common, especially in the first few weeks, and they ease with time. A few things make it easier:

- Go easy on alcohol early on, as it's a common trigger.
- Some people find coffee affects them more after they quit, so try cutting back.
- Keep busy, and change the routines you link with smoking.
- Put aside the money you save, and reward yourself with it.

### What if I slip up?

A slip doesn't mean you've failed, and it happens to most people who quit. Look at what triggered it, adjust your plan and keep going. Quitline can help you get back on track.

## What about quitting vaping?

Quitting vaping follows the same steps: a quit date, a plan for triggers and cravings, support from Quitline, and NRT to help with nicotine dependence. Quit's guide to setting a quit date covers both smoking and vaping.

## When to see a GP

Your GP can talk to you about other options and ongoing support, especially if you've tried to quit before. Talk to your GP or pharmacist first if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, or you have a heart condition, and let them know what medicines you take, because stopping smoking can change how some medicines work. The GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.
