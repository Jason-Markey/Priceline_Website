The best way to treat a blister is to leave it unpopped and protect it from rubbing. The skin over the top is a natural barrier against infection, and most blisters heal on their own. Cover it with a blister plaster or a padded dressing, change the shoes that caused it, and keep it clean. See a doctor if it looks infected, or straight away if you have diabetes or poor circulation and get a blister on your foot.

## How do I treat a blister?

1. **Leave it intact if you can.** Don't pop it or peel off the skin.
2. **Cover it.** A hydrocolloid blister plaster cushions the blister and keeps it clean while it heals. A dressing with a padded centre works too.
3. **Stop the rubbing.** Wear different shoes, or protect the spot, until it heals.
4. **If it's burst,** wash it gently with soap and water, leave the loose skin in place and cover it with a dressing.

If a large blister is very painful and in an awkward spot, talk to a pharmacist before draining it yourself. healthdirect advises seeing a doctor if you've drained a blister and the irritation, redness, warmth or pain gets worse. Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

## When should I see a doctor about a blister?

healthdirect advises seeing a doctor if:

- a blister shows signs of infection, such as pus, swelling, redness, warmth or increasing pain;
- the blister came from a burn or a severe sunburn;
- you have blisters in your mouth, on your eyes or on your genitals;
- blisters appear without an obvious cause;
- you have diabetes or problems with your circulation.

## Diabetes and your feet in summer

Diabetes can reduce the feeling and blood flow in your feet, so a blister can turn into an ulcer or infection without you noticing. Diabetes Australia says checking your feet every day matters even more in hot weather, when sweating raises the risk of blisters and ulcers.

- **Check your feet every day,** including between your toes and the soles, for blisters, cuts, redness, swelling or cracks. A mirror helps.
- **Get a blister, cut or ulcer seen the same day,** along with any unusual redness or swelling. Diabetes Australia advises getting cracked skin, corns and calluses seen within 7 days.
- **Protect your feet** with good, well-fitting shoes and socks.
- **Moisturise every day, but not between your toes,** and keep the skin between your toes dry.
- **See your GP or a podiatrist at least once a year** for a full foot check.

## Summer foot care on the Gold Coast

### Thongs

Thongs are handy for the beach, hot sand and shared showers, but they give your feet very little support. After a long day walking around a theme park or the shops in thongs, sore arches and heels are common. For a big day on your feet, wear supportive sandals or runners with socks.

### Hot sand and footpaths

Dry sand, boardwalks and footpaths can get hot enough to burn bare feet in the middle of the day. Wear thongs to the water's edge, and keep an eye on children's feet.

### Sunburnt feet

The tops of your feet are easy to miss with sunscreen and burn quickly at the beach. Cancer Council Queensland recommends SPF50 or SPF50+ broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen, so put it on your feet too, and reapply it after swimming.

### Cracked heels

Heat, sweat and open shoes can dry out the skin on your heels until it cracks. To soften them:

- moisturise your heels every day with a foot cream; creams containing urea help soften thick, dry skin;
- after a shower, gently smooth hard skin with a pumice stone or foot file;
- give your heels a break in closed shoes and socks some days.

See a podiatrist if your cracks are deep, painful or bleeding.

### Athlete's foot

Hot, humid weather and shared showers make athlete's foot (tinea) more common. Dry carefully between your toes, wear thongs in shared showers and change out of sweaty socks. Our guide to [athlete's foot treatment](/athletes-foot-treatment/) explains your options.

## How can I prevent blisters?

- **Break in new shoes** before a long walk or a big day out. Our [back to school checklist](/back-to-school-health-checklist/) has tips for new school shoes.
- **Wear moisture-wicking socks** that fit well, and keep your feet dry.
- **Protect hot spots early.** Put a blister plaster or an anti-chafe balm on any spot that starts to rub, before it blisters. Our guide to [chafing](/chafing-prevention-treatment/) has more.
