Most bites and stings cause pain, redness and swelling that settle with simple first aid: wash the area, hold a cold pack on it and take pain relief if you need it. The two exceptions to know are a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) and a bite from a big black spider that could be a funnel-web. For both, call 000 straight away. Here's what to do for each, from bees and fire ants to paralysis ticks.

## Signs of a severe allergic reaction: call 000

The Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy (ASCIA) lists these signs of anaphylaxis:

- difficult or noisy breathing
- swelling of the tongue
- swelling or tightness in the throat
- difficulty talking or a hoarse voice
- wheeze or a cough that won't stop
- dizziness that won't go away, or collapse
- in young children, looking pale and floppy

Hives, swelling of the face or a tingling mouth can come first, but not always.

**What to do:**

1. Lay the person flat and don't let them stand or walk. If breathing is hard, they can sit with their legs out in front of them.
2. If they have an adrenaline injector, give it now, following their action plan. If you're not sure, give it.
3. Call 000 for an ambulance.
4. If there's no improvement after 5 minutes, a second dose may be given if one is available.
5. Start CPR if the person stops responding and isn't breathing normally.

## How do I treat a bee sting?

1. **Get the sting out quickly.** A honey bee leaves its sting in the skin. Scrape it out sideways with a fingernail or the edge of a card, rather than squeezing it with tweezers.
2. **Wash the area** with soap and water.
3. **Hold a cold pack on it,** wrapped in a cloth, to ease the pain and swelling.
4. **Take pain relief** such as paracetamol if you need it. A pharmacist can suggest something for itching or swelling.

Watch for signs of anaphylaxis afterwards. Some people get a large area of swelling around a sting over the next day or so. If that happens, or if you've had a big reaction before, talk to a pharmacist or GP.

## What about wasp stings?

Wasps don't leave their sting behind and can sting more than once, so move away from the nest first. Then wash the area, apply a cold pack and take pain relief if you need it, the same as for a bee sting.

## Ant stings, including fire ants

Most ant stings cause a burning pain and a small red lump. Wash the area and apply a cold pack.

**Fire ants** are a particular concern in South East Queensland, where an eradication program runs from the Lockyer Valley to the Gold Coast. They're small, from 2 to 6 mm long, coppery brown with a darker abdomen, and they build mounds of loose soil. The National Fire Ant Eradication Program says a fire ant sting:

- burns at first, then forms small, itchy pustules within a couple of hours to two days;
- can take up to 10 days to heal.

Wash the stings with soap and water, use a cold compress for pain and swelling, and ask a pharmacist about an antihistamine for the itch. Don't burst the pustules, because they can get infected. See a doctor if the stings become redder and more painful a few days later, and call 000 for any signs of anaphylaxis.

**Report suspected fire ants within 24 hours.** Queenslanders are legally required to report them: call 13 25 23 or use the form at [fireants.org.au](https://www.fireants.org.au/).

## What should I do about a spider bite?

Most spider bites cause local pain, redness and swelling. Wash the area and apply a cold pack.

### Redback spider bites

- Apply a cold pack or ice, wrapped in a cloth, for up to 20 minutes at a time.
- **Don't** use a pressure bandage.
- Get medical help straight away for a young child or an older person, if the pain is severe or spreading, or if the person starts sweating or feeling unwell.

The Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26) can advise you any time.

### Funnel-web and other big black spiders

Treat a bite from any big black spider as a possible funnel-web bite and a medical emergency:

1. Call 000.
2. Keep the person lying down and as still as possible.
3. Apply a pressure immobilisation bandage: a firm elastic bandage over the bite, then a second one wrapped up the whole limb, starting from the fingers or toes. Splint the limb if you can.
4. Don't try to catch the spider.

## How do I remove a tick?

Paralysis ticks live along Australia's east coast, including the bushland around the Gold Coast. Australian advice is **"freeze it, don't squeeze it"**:

1. Kill the tick where it is, using an ether-containing spray made for killing ticks (ask us).
2. Leave it to drop off.
3. Then clean the area with soap and water or an antiseptic.

Don't pull a live tick off with tweezers, and don't put methylated spirits, kerosene or petroleum jelly on it: disturbing a tick can make it inject more of its saliva. If you can't freeze the tick or it won't drop off, leave it in place and see a doctor. Read the label and ask a pharmacist before using a tick spray on a young child.

If you're allergic to ticks, don't try to remove one yourself. Get medical help to have it removed, and call 000 for any trouble breathing.

**Ticks and children.** On children, ticks are often found on the head or chest. Check their scalp, behind the ears, the neck and chest after time in the bush. See a doctor urgently if a child becomes unsteady, weak or drowsy, or their face droops, after a tick bite. Paralysis can keep spreading for up to 48 hours after the tick has been removed.

## How can I avoid bites and stings?

- Wear shoes outside, especially on grass and around garden beds.
- Use insect repellent, and wear long, light-coloured clothing tucked in when you're in the bush.
- Wear gloves when gardening, and shake out shoes, gloves and clothes left outside.
- Keep sweet drinks covered outdoors, and check cans before you sip.
- Check for ticks after bushwalks.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

See a pharmacist or GP if a bite or sting shows signs of infection, such as spreading redness, warmth, pus or a fever, if swelling keeps growing, or if you're not sure what bit you. The GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.

**Call 000** for any signs of anaphylaxis, a bite from a big black spider, or a child who becomes unsteady or weak after a tick bite.
