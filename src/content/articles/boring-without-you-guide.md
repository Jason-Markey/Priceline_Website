Boring Without You, often shortened to BWU, is an Australian skincare brand made for one skin type: combination skin, where your T-zone (forehead, nose and chin) gets oily but your cheeks are normal or dry. The range is small: a gentle gel cleanser, a hydrating milk serum, a gel moisturiser and a two-in-one mask. Add an SPF50+ sunscreen every morning and you have a complete routine.

Here's how we'd explain it at the counter.

## What is Boring Without You?

Boring Without You was founded by cosmetic scientist Davey Rooney, a New Zealander based in Melbourne, and it focuses only on combination skin. It's sold nationally through Priceline.

## What is combination skin?

Combination skin has more than one skin type on the same face. Usually the T-zone is oily and shiny, with bigger-looking pores and the odd blackhead, while the cheeks and jaw are normal, dry or easily irritated.

That's why one product rarely suits the whole face. A strong cleanser or oil-control product that suits your nose can leave your cheeks tight and flaky, while a rich cream for dry cheeks can feel heavy on your forehead. On the Gold Coast, humidity can make the T-zone oilier in summer, and air-conditioning can dry out the rest of your face. Our guide to [skincare in humid weather](/skincare-humid-weather/) has more tips.

Glycerin is a humectant, which means it draws water into the top layer of the skin. Light, water-based textures like the serum and gel moisturiser are a good fit for combination skin, because they hydrate drier areas without feeling greasy on oily ones.

The multi-mask is the brand's answer to treating two skin types at once. The T-zone side is designed to absorb oil and contains a BHA (salicylic acid), which DermNet explains helps loosen the dead skin cells that block pores. Use it once or twice a week rather than every day.

## A simple routine for combination skin

In the morning:

1. Cleanse with a gentle gel cleanser.
2. Apply a hydrating serum.
3. Apply a light gel moisturiser, more on dry areas and less on your T-zone.
4. Finish with an SPF50+ sunscreen. Our guide to the [best sunscreen for the Gold Coast](/best-sunscreen-gold-coast/) can help you find a light, non-greasy one.

At night:

1. Cleanse to remove sunscreen and make-up.
2. Apply your serum and moisturiser.
3. Once or twice a week, use the multi-mask, putting each formula where it's needed.

Not sure what order to use other products in? See our [skincare routine order guide](/skincare-routine-order/).

## Tips for combination skin

- **Don't over-cleanse.** Washing more than twice a day or using harsh, foaming cleansers can leave your cheeks dry and irritated without making your T-zone less oily.
- **Use blotting papers** rather than extra washing if your T-zone gets shiny during the day.
- **Choose non-comedogenic products** (products that are less likely to block pores), especially sunscreen and make-up.
- **Add active ingredients slowly.** If you want to try retinol or acids, introduce one at a time and use them only where you need them. Our [retinol for beginners guide](/retinol-for-beginners/) explains how.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

- Breakouts are painful, leaving marks or scars, or not improving after 2 to 3 months. Our guide to [acne treatment at the pharmacy](/acne-treatment-pharmacy/) explains your options.
- Your cheeks are red, flushed or stinging often, which can be a sign of rosacea.
- A product causes burning, swelling or a rash that doesn't settle when you stop using it.
- You have eczema or patches of very dry, itchy skin.
