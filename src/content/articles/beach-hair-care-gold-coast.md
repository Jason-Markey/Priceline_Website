If you live anywhere near Broadbeach, your hair probably knows the Gold Coast climate better than you do. Between salt water, humidity and near-year-round UV, local hair takes a different kind of beating than hair anywhere else in the country. The team at Priceline Pacific Fair gets asked about beach hair damage almost daily, so we've put together answers to the questions we hear most.

## Why does Gold Coast humidity and salt water damage my hair so much?

Salt water strips natural oils from the hair shaft and roughens the outer cuticle, while our humidity swells the hair and makes it harder for moisture to stay locked in. Add near-constant UV exposure on top of that, and hair proteins break down faster than they would in a cooler, drier climate. It's a combination most Gold Coast locals deal with for a good eight or nine months of the year.

## What's the best way to protect my hair from the sun before a day at the beach?

A UV-protectant hair mist or leave-in spray applied before you head down to Broadbeach is the single easiest habit to build. Look for one with UV filters plus a lightweight oil (argan or coconut are common) so it also acts as a barrier against salt. A wide-brim hat is still the most effective sun protection you own, and it doubles as protection for your scalp, which burns more easily than most people realise.

## Should I use a leave-in conditioner or hair oil after I swim?

Yes — rinsing with fresh water as soon as possible after a swim removes most of the salt, and following up with a leave-in conditioner or a few drops of hair oil while it's still damp helps reseal the cuticle before it dries out in the sun. Skipping this step is usually what leads to that straw-like texture by the end of summer.

## How can I stop my hair frizzing in the humidity?

Frizz is really just moisture from the air getting into hair that isn't smooth or hydrated enough to resist it. A weekly deep-conditioning treatment, plus a lightweight anti-humidity serum on drier days, makes the biggest difference. Overwashing tends to make frizz worse, not better, because it strips the natural oils that would otherwise help seal the cuticle.

## Can a vitamin or supplement actually help with hair health?

Hair needs a steady supply of protein, iron, zinc and biotin to grow well, and a lot of Gold Coast locals are also outdoors enough that overall nutrient needs run a bit higher. ## How often should I wash beach hair without stripping it?

Most people swimming regularly over summer do well washing every second or third day with a sulphate-free shampoo, using plain water or a light rinse in between. This keeps enough natural oil in the hair to buffer against sun and salt, while still clearing out chlorine or salt residue before it builds up.

## Is it worth getting a trim before peak swimming season?

Generally yes. Sun and salt damage shows up first as split ends, and a trim now stops that damage travelling further up the hair shaft over summer. It's a small thing, but it makes every other step in this list work better.
