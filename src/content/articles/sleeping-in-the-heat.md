To sleep better on a hot night, keep the sun out of your bedroom during the day, let cooler air in once it's cooler outside than in, and use a fan or air-conditioning if you have one. A cool shower before bed, light cotton sleepwear and bedding, and a glass of water by the bed all help. If you can't sleep, get up and do something quiet until you feel drowsy, rather than lying there getting hotter and more frustrated.

## Why is it hard to sleep when it's hot?

Your body temperature drops a little as you fall asleep. On a hot, humid night that's harder to do: sweat doesn't evaporate as easily in humid air, so you stay warm, take longer to fall asleep and wake more often. Queensland Health also points out that hot nights stop your body recovering from the heat of the day, which is part of why heatwaves are hard on health.

## How do I keep my bedroom cool?

- **Shut out the sun during the day.** Close curtains and blinds, especially on windows that get the afternoon sun.
- **Open up once it's cooler outside than in.** Keep windows closed while it's hotter outside, then open them in the evening to let the breeze through.
- **Use a fan or air-conditioning.** A fan moves air across your skin and helps sweat evaporate. Air-conditioning or evaporative cooling works better, but costs more to run.
- **Choose natural fabrics.** Light cotton or linen sheets are cooler than synthetic ones. Keep a light sheet or doona nearby: your body temperature is at its lowest in the early hours of the morning.
- **Sleep somewhere cooler** if you can. Heat rises, so a downstairs room is often cooler than an upstairs one.

## How do I cool my body down?

- **Have a cool shower or bath** before bed.
- **Cool your hands, feet and face.** A cold, damp face washer, or soaking your feet in cool water, helps you cool down.
- **Wear loose, light sleepwear** in a breathable fabric, or less.
- **Keep a glass of cold water by the bed.**

## Drink enough during the day

Queensland Health advises drinking fluids, preferably cool water, through the day rather than waiting until you're thirsty. Your urine should be clear or a light straw colour. Go easy on alcohol: it can make you dehydrated and disturb your sleep. Our guide to [heat exhaustion](/heat-exhaustion-symptoms/) has more on staying hydrated in a Gold Coast summer.

## Babies and young children in the heat

Red Nose recommends dressing your baby for the temperature of the room, rather than trying to change the temperature of the room:

- In hot weather, a lightweight sleeping bag with just a nappy underneath may be all your baby needs. Choose a sleeping bag rated for the room temperature.
- If you wrap your baby, use a light cotton or muslin wrap over a nappy and singlet.
- Take off hats, bonnets and hoods for sleep, and keep your baby's head and face uncovered.
- Feel your baby's chest or the back of their neck to check their temperature: it should feel warm. Cool hands and feet are normal.
- A fan can help on very hot days.

Our guide to [sun protection for babies](/baby-sun-protection/) covers time outdoors.

## Older people, medicines and hot nights

Queensland Health says babies, older people, pregnant and breastfeeding women, people with medical conditions and people taking certain medicines are most at risk in the heat. If you have an older relative or neighbour living alone, check on them during a heatwave.

Some medicines affect how your body copes with heat, or how much fluid you should drink. Don't stop a medicine, but ask a pharmacist if you're not sure about yours. Medicines need protecting from the heat too: many are labelled to be stored below 25°C, so follow the storage directions on the pack and don't leave them in a hot car or on a sunny windowsill.

## What if I still can't sleep?

- **Wait until you feel drowsy** before you go to bed, and get up if you can't sleep.
- **Keep the same wake-up time,** even after a bad night.
- **Avoid long naps** during the day.
- **Don't rely on alcohol** to help you sleep.

Talk to a pharmacist before taking anything to help you sleep, because some sleep aids interact with other medicines or aren't suitable for older people. Our guide to [trouble sleeping](/trouble-sleeping-tips/) has more tips.

## How can our pharmacists help?

Our pharmacists at Pacific Fair can check whether any of your medicines affect how you handle the heat, help you store them safely, and talk you through sleep problems. If you take several medicines, a [medication review](/medication-reviews/) can check they're all still working well for you.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

See your GP if poor sleep is affecting your days for more than a few weeks, if you snore loudly or stop breathing in your sleep, or if you feel unwell in the heat. The GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), an independent general practice located inside our store, can see you.

**Call 000** if someone is confused, collapses or has a seizure in the heat. These can be signs of heatstroke, which is a medical emergency.
