Start by spraying a few fragrances onto testing cards, then try your favourite on your skin and give it a few hours before you decide. A perfume changes as it settles, so the scent you smell in the first minute isn't the one you'll be wearing all afternoon.

Taking your time pays off, because once the seal is broken, a fragrance can't be returned just because you've changed your mind. Here's a simple way to test, and what to know about returns before you buy.

## Why does a perfume smell different after an hour?

Most perfumes are built in layers that reveal themselves over time. DermNet describes three:

| Notes | What they are | When you notice them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top (head) notes | Light notes such as citrus, light fruits and herbs | Straight away. They usually evaporate within 5 to 30 minutes |
| Heart (middle) notes | The main body of the scent | From about 20 to 60 minutes, usually lasting 2 to 4 hours |
| Base notes | Deeper notes such as cedarwood, sandalwood, vanilla and musk | From about 30 minutes, or as the scent dries down. Some last more than 24 hours |

That's why a quick sniff can mislead. The bright opening that won you over may be gone within half an hour, leaving the heart and base notes you'll actually live with.

## How do I test a perfume properly?

1. **Start on testing cards.** The testers are out in our fragrance cabinets, and our team can spray the ones you like onto testing cards. Write the name on each card, so you don't mix them up.
2. **Narrow it down.** Smell the cards again after a few minutes and set aside any you're unsure about. Aim for a shortlist of one or two.
3. **Try your favourite on skin.** A card shows you the fragrance; your skin shows you how it wears on you. Spray once on the inside of your wrist or forearm, holding the bottle a little way from your skin. Comparing two? Put one on each arm.
4. **Don't rub.** Rubbing can break down the scent before it settles. Let it dry on its own.
5. **Walk away and wait.** Smell your skin again after 30 minutes, after an hour and after a few hours. Do you still like it? How long does it last?
6. **Take a card home.** Still deciding? We can spray the fragrance on a testing card for you to take home, so you can see how you feel about it the next day.

## How many perfumes can you test at once?

Fewer than you might think, because your nose gets used to smells quickly. After a few sprays, it gets harder to tell scents apart.

A few practical rules:

- Keep it to three or four cards at a time.
- Try no more than two on your skin, one on each arm.
- Take a break. Step away from the fragrance area for a few minutes, then come back to your shortlist.
- If everything starts to smell the same, come back another day.

## Which concentration should you test?

The same fragrance often comes in more than one strength. In general terms, from strongest to lightest, DermNet describes them like this:

- **Parfum (or extrait):** the most concentrated, and usually the longest lasting.
- **Eau de parfum:** the most common type, lasting about 4 to 5 hours.
- **Eau de toilette:** lighter and often worn by day, lasting about 2 to 3 hours.
- **Eau de cologne:** lighter again, lasting about 2 hours.

Check which version the tester is, and test the one you plan to buy.

## Does Gold Coast heat change things?

Test in the conditions you'll wear it in. If you'll mostly wear a scent outdoors on warm days, spend some time outside with your skin test rather than judging it in air-conditioning. ## Buying perfume as a Christmas gift?

You can't test a gift on the person who'll wear it, so work from clues:

- Start with what they already wear. A photo of the bottle on their dresser is a great starting point.
- Think about the scent family they like: fresh, floral, warm or woody. You can [shop fragrance by scent on priceline.com.au](https://www.priceline.com.au/c/fragrance-shop-by-scent).
- Keep the receipt, and suggest they keep the seal intact until they're sure, in case it needs to come back. Change-of-mind returns are 30 days from the day you buy, so keep that in mind if you're shopping early (see the returns rules below).

## What if fragrance bothers your skin?

If perfume has irritated your skin before, stick to testing cards in store, and read our guide to [fragrance and sensitive skin](/fragrance-sensitive-skin/) before you try anything on your skin. You can also ask our pharmacists.

## Can you return perfume if you change your mind?

Only if it's unopened. Under Priceline's Returns and Exchange Policy, you can return fragrance within 30 days with proof of purchase, as long as it's unopened and in its original sealed packaging. If it came as part of a gift-with-purchase or bundle offer, the items need to come back together.

Once the seal is broken, a fragrance can't be returned just because it isn't the scent you hoped for. That's in line with the law: the Queensland Government's Office of Fair Trading explains that businesses don't have to give a refund or exchange if you simply change your mind, although some choose to.

Faulty products are different. Your rights under the Australian Consumer Law apply, and the Office of Fair Trading notes you're always entitled to a refund, repair or replacement if a consumer guarantee isn't met. Either way, keep your receipt.
