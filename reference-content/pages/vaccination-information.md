## Free government-funded vaccines, with no service fee

Vaccines under the National Immunisation Program (NIP) are free for eligible people with a Medicare card, and we don't charge a service fee to give them. Eligibility depends on your age, pregnancy, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander status and some medical conditions. The main groups are:

-   **Flu:** children aged 6 months to under 5, pregnant women, people aged 65 and over, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, and people with certain medical conditions.
-   **Whooping cough:** pregnant women, ideally between 20 and 32 weeks of each pregnancy.
-   **Shingles:** people aged 65 and over, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 50 and over, and people aged 18 and over at higher risk because of a medical condition or treatment.
-   **Pneumococcal:** people aged 65 and over, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 25 and over, and adults with certain medical conditions.
-   **COVID-19:** free for everyone in Australia, in line with current advice.

Not sure if you're eligible? Ask us and we'll check for you, or see the [National Immunisation Program](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/national-immunisation-program). If you're not eligible, a private fee applies; you'll see current prices when you book online.

## Who can we vaccinate?

Our pharmacists vaccinate adults and children aged 5 and over. For younger children, the GPs at [Help Medical](/help-medical/), inside our store, can help.

## Walk in or book: how it works

1.  **Walk in or book.** Scan the QR code at the counter to join the queue, or [book a time online](https://healthengine.com.au/pharmacy/qld/broadbeach/priceline-pharmacy-pacific-fair/s54055).
2.  **Quick check.** We'll ask a few questions about your health, allergies and previous vaccines.
3.  **Your vaccine.** It's given in our private consultation area.
4.  **Stay for 15 minutes.** We'll ask you to wait in store for 15 minutes afterwards, just in case of a reaction.
5.  **Recorded for you.** Your vaccination is recorded on the Australian Immunisation Register.

## What to bring

-   Your Medicare card, if you have one
-   Details of any recent vaccines, if you know them
-   A list of your current medicines

## Travelling overseas?

Some travel vaccines need several doses or take time to work, so start planning 6 to 8 weeks before you fly if you can. See our [travel health](/travel-health/) page.

## Vaccinations in pregnancy

Whooping cough and flu vaccines are recommended during pregnancy to protect you and your baby in the first months of life, and both are free under the NIP. Talk to your pharmacist, GP or midwife about timing.

## Common questions

### Can I have more than one vaccine at the same visit?

Often, yes. Your pharmacist will check which vaccines can be given together.

### What if I feel unwell on the day?

A mild cold usually isn't a problem. If you have a fever, it's best to wait until you're well. Ask us if you're not sure.

### Are there side effects?

A sore arm, tiredness or a mild temperature for a day or two are common. If you're worried about any reaction after leaving the store, contact your GP. In an emergency, call 000.
