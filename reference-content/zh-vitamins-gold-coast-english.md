### How do I contact you about a bulk purchase?
If you plan to buy a lot of vitamins or supplements at once, it's best to contact us a few days ahead so we can get the stock in. By phone or email, tell us:
- the product names (a photo helps) and quantities
- the day you expect to visit
- whether you need postage, and the country or city it's going to

Bulk purchases may be discounted, depending on the products and quantities. We can also help arrange postage and tell you the options and cost.

### Which vitamins and supplements do you have?
Multivitamins and single vitamins and minerals (for example calcium, magnesium, vitamin C and vitamin D), pregnancy and pre-conception supplements, children's vitamins, fish oil (omega-3) and probiotics. Common Australian brands include Blackmores, Swisse, Nature's Way, Nature's Own, Bioglan, Cenovis, Ostelin, BioCeuticals, Herbs of Gold and Ethical Nutrients (the same list as the English vitamins page). Stock changes, so please check ahead for bulk orders.

### What should I look for when choosing?
- **The AUST L or AUST R number on the label.** It shows the product is entered on the TGA's register (ARTG) and can be legally sold in Australia. Most vitamins are AUST L (lower-risk "listed" products).
- **The expiry date.** For gifts or products you'll keep for a while, choose a longer expiry; we can check dates for you.
- **Who it's for.** If the person is pregnant, breastfeeding, a child, older, or takes prescription medicines, ask a pharmacist first, because some supplements can interact with medicines. Pharmacist advice is free, no appointment needed.
- **Buying in bulk doesn't mean taking more.** Follow the directions on the label and don't take more than the recommended dose.

### What is Lucas' Papaw Ointment?
Lucas' Papaw Ointment ("Australian papaw ointment") is a Queensland classic, developed more than a century ago by Brisbane doctor Thomas Pennington Lucas and still made in Brisbane by his family's company. Its key ingredient is fermented papaw.
- The label lists uses including minor burns, cuts, insect bites, nappy rash and chafing. For a minor burn, cool it under cool running water for 20 minutes first.
- For a serious burn or wound, or if a wound looks infected, see a doctor.
- Sizes range from a small lip applicator tube to a large jar, which makes it a good gift; check what's in stock.
- On international flights, creams and ointments in hand luggage are usually limited to 100 mL each, so pack large jars in checked luggage.
Always read the label and follow the directions for use.

### How do I choose lanolin cream?
Lanolin is a natural oil from sheep's wool, and Australian lanolin cream is a popular moisturiser and gift. It's thick and rich, so a little goes a long way, and suits dry hands, elbows and feet.
Lanolin allergy is uncommon, but people with eczema or broken skin are more likely to react. Try it on a small patch of skin first, and stop using it if it goes red or itchy.

### Can I get a tax refund?
If you're leaving Australia within 60 days and spend AU$300 or more (GST included, can be over several purchases) in our store, you can usually claim the GST back at the airport through the Tourist Refund Scheme (TRS).
- Ask us for a tax invoice when you pay. Invoices of AU$1,000 or more need your full name as on your passport.
- Keep the goods unopened and carry them in your hand luggage so the Australian Border Force (ABF) can inspect them. Larger liquids, sprays or gels can go in checked luggage, but show them to ABF before you check in.
- Claim at the TRS counter at the international airport; claims close 30 minutes before departure, so allow plenty of time. You can fill in your details beforehand in the TRS app.
- Most vitamins, skincare and fragrance include GST; prescription medicines don't, so they can't be claimed.
The ABF website has the full rules (linked).

### Can I buy prescription medicines?
Prescription medicines need a prescription from an Australian doctor; overseas prescriptions can't be dispensed in Australia. Help Medical, an independent general practice inside our store, can see visitors (visitors without Medicare pay a fee), and you can fill the prescription at our pharmacy straight away.
